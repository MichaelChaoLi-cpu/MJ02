# Candidate Reformulation: Monsoon Structure, Post-Monsoon Vegetation, and Food Consumption

Status: **candidate for human approval; not yet the authoritative AnaSOP direction**

Research type: **applied**

## Recommended Working Title

Rainfall totals conceal phase-specific ecological and food-consumption risks from Cambodia's
changing monsoon structure

## Central Research Question

Do the timing and compound structure of Cambodia's monsoon reveal ecological and household food-
consumption losses that seasonal rainfall totals alone miss?

## Supporting Questions

1. Do longer intraseasonal dry spells reduce post-monsoon vegetation condition after holding total
   May-October rainfall constant?
2. Do compound hot-dry seasons reduce real food consumption per household member after holding
   rainfall, onset timing, and dry-spell duration constant?
3. Are both relationships stable across alternative monsoon definitions, spatial scales, sensors,
   survey waves, fixed-effect structures, and temporal placebo tests?
4. Where do recurrent dry-spell and compound hot-dry risks overlap nationally, and where can the
   survey evidence be transported without unsupported extrapolation?

## Evidence Already Established

### Post-monsoon ecological response

- At the primary 5 km radius, a one-standard-deviation longer intraseasonal dry spell is associated
  with a 0.0467-SD lower November-February EVI anomaly and a 0.0556-SD lower NDVI anomaly.
- The dry-spell coefficient is negative for both sensors at 2, 5, and 10 km and under both frozen
  monsoon definitions.
- The EVI coefficient remains negative in every leave-one-temporal-block-out specification.
- Adding monsoon structure to rainfall totals improves strict spatial-temporal out-of-fold RMSE by
  1.15% for post-monsoon EVI.

### Household food-consumption response

- A one-standard-deviation increase in the last completed season's compound hot-dry day anomaly is
  associated with 3.70% lower real food consumption per household member.
- The primary 95% log-point interval is -0.0568 to -0.0187.
- The coefficient remains negative at 2, 5, and 10 km, under both monsoon definitions, after
  household-composition adjustment, and with repeated-village fixed effects.
- Every leave-one-CSES-wave-out estimate is negative.
- In clean interview months, the current-season coefficient remains negative when the future season
  is included simultaneously, while the future-season interval contains zero.

### Buffering result

- Irrigable parcels, historical road access, and baseline settlement connectivity do not
  significantly modify the hot-dry food-consumption relationship after Holm correction.
- These null interactions belong in the Appendix. They do not justify an adaptation-effect claim.

## Claims Not Supported

- The same-calendar-year annual-NPP model does not show that monsoon structure adds predictive
  information beyond rainfall totals.
- The full May-February EVI model also fails its primary incremental-prediction gate.
- The data do not support a single climate-to-NPP-to-household mediation chain.
- The design does not identify irrigation, roads, or settlement connectivity as causal protective
  interventions.
- EVI and NDVI measure vegetation condition, not crop yield; the household result measures food
  consumption, not nutritional status.

## Interpretation

The defensible contribution is not that every monsoon disruption follows one causal chain. It is
that rainfall totals collapse distinct seasonal processes: dry spells leave a robust post-monsoon
vegetation penalty, while compound hot-dry exposure predicts a separate and robust household food-
consumption penalty. The national satellite panel establishes when and where ecological stress
emerges; the survey establishes a human welfare consequence of the same broader monsoon-structure
family. The paper should use associational language and emphasize temporal ordering, fixed effects,
cross-sensor replication, cross-scale stability, and placebo behavior rather than mediation.
